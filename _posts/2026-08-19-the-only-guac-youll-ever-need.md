**Serves:** 2 to 4. Scale up if you're feeding a crowd, and be smart about it.

> A quick heads up before we start: if you don't like raw onion or cilantro, this recipe is not for you. No hard feelings, just... move along.

A note on the amounts below. They are the safe version. They are what I write down when I'm handing the recipe to someone who has to trust me sight unseen, and they will make very good guacamole. They are not what I actually do.

What I actually do is more jalapeño, more garlic powder, and more lime than any of this says. Both columns are below. Start in the left one. Work your way right when you're ready.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | As written | How I actually make it |
|---|---|---|
| Ripe avocados | 4 to 6 large | same |
| Vine-ripe tomatoes | 1 to 2 medium, seeds and liquid removed, ¼-inch dice | same |
| Purple onion | ½ small-medium, ¼-inch dice | same, to taste |
| Cilantro leaves | ¼ to ⅓ cup, stems removed, finely chopped | same |
| **Jalapeño** | 1, finely diced to ⅛-inch or smaller, start with half | **2, and I'm not starting with half** |
| Ground cayenne | a pinch or two | a pinch or two |
| **Garlic powder** | 1 tsp | **2 to 3 tsp, and never garlic salt** |
| **Lime** | ¼, juiced | **½ to a whole one, juiced** |
| Lemon juice | 1 tsp, optional, slows browning | optional |
| Flaky sea salt | to taste, Maldon preferred | to taste, Maldon |

## Instructions

**1. Prep the avocados.**
Check ripeness by pulling off the little stem cap. Underneath should be brown. Scoop into a bowl big enough for the whole batch, and err large, because nobody wants to dirty a second bowl. Break the avocado up roughly with a whisk or fork until the pieces are about ¼-inch. Don't go smaller. You can't undo over-mashed avocado.

**2. Add the tomatoes.**
Dice into ¼-inch cubes, same as the avocado, and add to the bowl. **Do not stir yet.**

**3. Add the onion.**
¼-inch dice again. Add about half the onion to start. You can always add more, but you can't take it out once it's in. Balance is the goal here, not overpowering the guac. **Still don't stir.**

**4. Add the cilantro.**
Pick the leaves, chop into a fine chiffonade, or as close as your knife skills allow. Add to the bowl. **Still not stirring. Patience.**

**5. Bring the heat.**
Jalapeño is the one thing that doesn't match. Go fine, ⅛-inch or smaller, even smaller if you can hold the knife steady, so the heat spreads evenly instead of arriving in ambushes. Add half to start, and more later if you want it spicier. Add the cayenne and the garlic powder.

This is the first place the safe version and the real version part ways. One jalapeño starting at half is a recipe that can't hurt you. Two, going in whole, is what's actually in the bowl at my house. Same with the garlic powder: 1 tsp is the printed number, 2 to 3 is the number. Do not substitute garlic salt for it, ever, at any quantity.

**6. Add the citrus.**
Squeeze the lime juice over everything, trying to spread it around the bowl, because it'll help things mix later. Add the lemon juice too if you want to slow browning. Browned guac is still totally fine to eat, by the way. Stir it and it goes away.

A quarter of a lime is the cautious pour. I use half to a whole one. The lime is doing more work than people give it credit for, and under-limed guacamole tastes flat in a way that's hard to diagnose after the fact.

**7. First stir.**
Grab a tablespoon and gently fold everything together, just enough to combine, not to smooth it out. You want a chunky texture, so stir a little, then stop. You'll repeat this in small passes rather than all at once.

**8. Taste and adjust.**
Grab a good corn tortilla chip, scoop up a bite with a bit of everything on it, and taste. Look for balance: a hint of lime, a little sweetness from the tomato, a slow burn from the cayenne, and a bit of bite from the jalapeño. Adjust onion, cilantro, or jalapeño as needed. Repeat as many times as it takes. Just keep folding gently so you don't smash the texture.

**9. Season.**
Finish with flaky sea salt, crumbled between your fingers as you sprinkle. Maldon is the move. Add a little, stir gently, taste with a chip, repeat until the salt and chip balance perfectly. If you don't have flaky salt, Himalayan pink is an acceptable backup. If you don't have that either, it might be time for a grocery run.

---

*Pro tip: this recipe rewards patience. Don't rush the stirring, and always taste with a chip, not a spoon.*
